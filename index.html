Who We Are

Chemistry of Atmospheric Pollutants Research Laboratory

We are a research group at the Indian Institute of Technology Bombay, dedicated to understanding the chemical composition, formation mechanisms, and transformation pathways of atmospheric pollutants — from individual molecules to city-scale pollution episodes.

Our research spans secondary aerosol formation, aerosol optical characterisation, indoor air quality, fog–aerosol interactions, tropospheric ozone chemistry, and long-term trends of aerosols in the atmosphere. We combine field measurements, laboratory experiments, satellite data, and atmospheric modelling to build a comprehensive picture of air pollution over India.

Our findings directly support evidence-based environmental policy, public health assessment, and sustainable urban planning — particularly in the context of rapidly growing South Asian cities. Reach us at caps.iitb@gmail.com.

Principal Investigator

Principal Investigator · Core Faculty
Prof. Abhishek Chakraborty
Assistant Professor · Environmental Science & Engineering Department, IIT Bombay

Prof. Chakraborty leads the Chemistry of Atmospheric Pollutants (CAP) Laboratory at IIT Bombay (Room ES 408, ESED-DESE Building). His doctoral work at IIT Kanpur focused on fog and organic aerosol interactions — their sources, composition, and chemistry. His research interests include atmospheric processing of organic aerosols, fog–smog–aerosol interactions, physicochemical characterisation and source apportionment of aerosols, real-time air pollution monitoring, and air pollution control technologies. He has been cited over 1,100 times and is an awardee of the Academic Excellence Award, IIT Kanpur.

What We Study

Research Areas

Our work spans the full lifecycle of air pollutants — from emission to deposition.

🌫️

Secondary Aerosol Formation

Investigating the formation pathways of secondary organic aerosols (SOA) and secondary inorganic aerosols from gas-phase precursors including VOCs, NOₓ, and SO₂ under varying atmospheric conditions.

🔭

Aerosol Optical Properties

Accessing and quantifying optical characteristics of aerosols — including scattering, absorption, and extinction coefficients — and understanding their role in radiative forcing and climate.

🌡️

Heatwaves & Tropospheric Ozone

Studying the interplay between extreme heat events, wildfire emissions, and tropospheric ozone chemistry, with a focus on how heatwaves amplify atmospheric pollutant formation.

🏠

Indoor Air Quality & Health

Characterising indoor aerosol chemistry and its impact on human health using GIS-based spatial analysis to map exposure patterns across urban environments.

📡

Air Quality during High Pollution Episodes

Ground-reanalysis of CPCB–CAMS data, secondary aerosol formation chemistry during high pollution episodes, and statistical and regression-based air quality analysis.

🔗

Emission & Air Quality Linkages in India

Establishing source–receptor relationships between emission inventories and observed air quality trends across Indian cities, informing mitigation policy.

🧪

Organo Nitrates & Organo Sulfates

Chemical characterisation of organo nitrates and organo sulfates as key products of atmospheric oxidation chemistry and their contribution to aerosol mass loading.

💨

Air Quality & Exposure Assessment

Integrated assessment of population exposure to fine particulate matter using atmospheric chemistry models, aerosol science, and air pollution modelling frameworks.

💧

Aqueous Processing & Fog–Aerosol Interactions

Exploring aqueous-phase chemistry in fog droplets and cloud water, and how fog–aerosol interactions alter the chemical composition and size distribution of atmospheric particles.

📏

Size-Resolved Secondary Aerosol Formation

Size-segregated analysis of secondary aerosol formation to understand how particle size governs reactivity, lifetime, deposition, and health impacts.

📈

Long-Term Trends of Aerosols

Analysing multi-year datasets to identify long-term trends in aerosol loading, chemical composition, and optical properties across the Indian subcontinent.

🌦️

Meteorological Influence on Pollutant Dispersion

Understanding how boundary layer dynamics, wind patterns, temperature inversions, and monsoon meteorology control the transport and accumulation of atmospheric pollutants.

Infrastructure

Lab Facilities & Instruments

Our laboratory is equipped with state-of-the-art analytical instruments for atmospheric research.

⚗️ ACSM

Provides near-real-time measurements of the chemical composition and mass concentrations of non-refractory submicron particulate matter.

🪨 CASS

Used to measure and characterize carbonaceous aerosol components, particularly organic carbon (OC) and elemental carbon (EC), in particulate matter.

🔥 OC-EC Analyzer

Used to measure organic carbon (OC) and elemental carbon (EC) in particulate matter, typically using thermal–optical analysis.

📉 Aethalometer (AE36)

Real-time measurement of black carbon and brown carbon concentrations with source apportionment capability.

👁️ Visibility Sensor

Instrument that measures atmospheric visibility by detecting the scattering of light caused by airborne particles and aerosols.

🌤️ Automatic Weather Station

Continuously measures and records meteorological parameters such as temperature, humidity, wind speed and direction, rainfall, and atmospheric pressure.
